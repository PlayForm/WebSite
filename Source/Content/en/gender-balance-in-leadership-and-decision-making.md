# GENDER BALANCE IN LEADERSHIP AND DECISION-MAKING POLICY

Effective date: 11.23.2026 / November 23rd 2026\
Last updated: 11.23.2026 / November 23rd 2026

This policy carries the leadership and decision-making part of the
[Gender Equality Plan]. It sets out who decides inside the Company, what composition
those bodies must reach, and the mechanisms that get them there without substituting
quotas for judgement.

## Purpose

Decisions about what the Company builds, who it hires and how it rewards people are
made in rooms, panels and committees. If those bodies are drawn from one group, the
decisions carry that group's blind spots - not through malice, but because no one in
the room can see what is missing. Balanced decision-making is therefore a quality
measure for the Company's work, as well as a matter of fairness.

The Company also competes for people. Organisations whose leadership visibly
includes more than one kind of person recruit from a wider pool, and the evidence
that a role is open to anyone is the composition of the roles above it.

## Scope

This policy applies to:

- the management team and any successor body;
- the Gender Equality Committee and any other standing committee;
- recruitment, promotion and pay-review panels;
- project steering groups, review boards and technical decision forums;
- the programme committees of Company-organised events;
- the Company's representation in consortia, associations and external bodies where
  the Company appoints the representative.

## The principle and the target

The principle is **balanced participation**: no body that decides should be composed
of a single sex, and each body should reach at least 40% of the under-represented sex.
The 40% figure follows the benchmark commonly used for balanced participation in
decision-making, and the Company treats 50% as the ambition wherever a body is
appointed rather than elected.

The target applies to the under-represented sex in that particular body, whichever
sex that is in that context. It is a composition target for the body, never a
criterion applied to an individual appointment: an individual is appointed on merit,
and the target is met by widening the pool from which the merit decision is made.

## Bodies covered

- **Management team** - the body that takes the Company's operating decisions.
- **Gender Equality Committee** - the body that governs this framework.
- **Recruitment and promotion panels** - the bodies that decide about people.
- **Project and technical forums** - the bodies that decide what gets built and how.
- **Event programme committees** - the bodies that decide who is heard.
- **External representation** - the people who speak for the Company outside it.

## Balanced shortlists and panels

- **Shortlists.** Where the candidate pool allows, every shortlist for a leadership
  role includes at least one candidate of each sex. Where it does not, the reason is
  recorded and the sourcing is reviewed before the appointment is made.
- **Panels.** No panel that decides on recruitment, promotion or pay is composed of a
  single sex where the Company can avoid it. Panel members are trained under the
  [Gender Equality Training and Awareness Programme] before they sit.
- **Same process for everyone.** Structured interviews, the same questions, a written
  scoring rubric agreed in advance, and scores recorded before discussion. See the
  [Recruitment and Career Progression Policy].
- **Internal first.** Leadership vacancies are published internally before an external
  search begins, so that people already inside the Company can see and pursue them.

## Sponsorship and succession

- Each member of the management team sponsors at least one person from an
  under-represented group. Sponsorship means advocating for the person in the rooms
  they are not in, and putting their name forward for stretch work - not mentoring
  advice alone.
- Every critical role has a named successor, and the succession map is reviewed
  annually for skew.
- Stretch assignments - the work that makes a promotion possible - are allocated by
  published criteria and tracked by sex, so that opportunity does not circulate
  through informal networks.
- People returning from parental leave are included in the succession map on the same
  terms as everyone else.

## Transparency of appointments

- Appointments to leadership roles are announced internally with the criteria that
  were applied and the composition of the panel.
- The composition of each decision-making body is published in the biennial
  monitoring report, by sex, without naming individuals where the number is too small
  to protect them.
- The Company publishes its representation target, so that missing it is visible.

## Meetings, events and public representation

- Events organised by the Company aim for 50% women among speakers, counted across
  the whole programme. A single-speaker event is not exempt from the principle, only
  from the arithmetic.
- Panels at Company events have more than one voice by default; a single-speaker
  format requires a stated reason.
- When the Company is invited to send a speaker or representative, it proposes a
  balanced slate rather than a single name.
- Meeting chairs rotate, so that chairing experience is distributed rather than
  accumulated.

## When a body is not balanced

A body below the target is not left to correct itself. The owner of that body brings
a remediation plan to the Gender Equality Committee, stating the cause - pool size,
timing, structure of the role, or the process that produced the composition - and the
action that will change it. The plan and its outcome are recorded in the minutes and
reported in the next monitoring report.

## Targets and indicators

- 30% of the under-represented sex in every decision-making body by 31 December 2027.
- 40% of the under-represented sex in the management team and in every
  decision-making body by 31 December 2028.
- 50% women among speakers at Company-organised events from 1 January 2027.
- 100% of leadership shortlists balanced where the pool allows, from 1 January 2027.
- Indicators: composition of each body by sex; share of balanced shortlists; share of
  balanced panels; share of women among speakers and chairs; share of sponsored people
  moving into a leadership role within two years; share of stretch assignments held by
  each sex.

# GENDER-BASED VIOLENCE AND SEXUAL HARASSMENT POLICY

Effective date: 11.23.2026 / November 23rd 2026\
Last updated: 11.23.2026 / November 23rd 2026

This policy carries the conduct and safety part of the
[Gender Equality Plan]. It states what the Company will not tolerate, how a person
can report what happened, what the Company will do with the report, and what happens
to the person responsible.

## Purpose and zero tolerance

The Company has **zero tolerance** for gender-based violence and sexual harassment.
Zero tolerance is a commitment about consequences, not about tone: a substantiated
case results in action, up to and including termination of the relationship with the
Company, and, where the conduct may be criminal, in a report to the competent
authorities.

The policy exists because the alternative - relying on informal resolution - puts the
burden on the person affected, rewards the person with more power, and leaves no
record from which the Company can learn.

## Definitions

- **Sexual harassment** - any form of unwanted verbal, non-verbal or physical conduct
  of a sexual nature, with the purpose or effect of violating a person's dignity, in
  particular when it creates an intimidating, hostile, degrading, humiliating or
  offensive environment.
- **Harassment related to sex or gender** - unwanted conduct related to a person's sex
  or gender, with the same purpose or effect, including comments about appearance,
  assumptions about competence, exclusion, and repeated belittling on gendered grounds.
- **Gender-based violence** - violence directed against a person because of their
  gender, or affecting persons of a particular gender disproportionately, including
  physical, psychological, sexual and economic harm, stalking and threats.
- **Online and technology-facilitated abuse** - the same conduct carried out through
  chat, email, video calls, social platforms, repositories, shared documents, or any
  other channel used for work, including the sharing or threatening to share intimate
  images.
- **Retaliation** - any adverse treatment of a person because they reported, supported
  a report, or took part in an investigation.
- **Third party** - a contractor, supplier, partner, event participant or visitor who
  is not employed by the Company.

Unwanted conduct is assessed from the perspective of the person subjected to it. The
intention of the person responsible does not determine whether the conduct was
harassment.

## Scope and settings

This policy applies to all personnel whatever their contract type, to candidates, to
contractors and partners, and to third parties in their dealings with the Company. It
covers conduct in:

- the workplace and any Company premises;
- remote work and any private space used for work;
- business travel, including transport, accommodation and meals;
- Company-organised events, off-sites and social activities, formal or informal;
- conferences and external events where a person attends as a representative of the
  Company;
- online channels used for work, including direct messages, calls and repositories.

Conduct outside these settings is addressed where it has a connection to work - for
example where a person in a position of power over a colleague uses that position, or
where the conduct affects the working environment.

## Prevention

- **Training.** Every person covered by this policy completes the module defined in the
  [Gender Equality Training and Awareness Programme] annually. Managers complete a
  specific module on recognising, responding to and escalating reports.
- **Code of conduct.** The Company's community
  [Code of Conduct](https://github.com/PlayForm/WebSite/blob/Current/CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md)
  applies to its open work, and this policy applies to its workplace.
- **Risk assessment.** Off-site events, travel and late-working arrangements are
  assessed for risk before they happen, including the composition of the group, the
  travel arrangements and the availability of a route home.
- **Contractual clause.** New contracts with contractors, suppliers and event partners
  from 1 January 2027 require them to meet the standard in this policy, and to inform
  the Company of any report concerning their personnel working for the Company.
- **Language and culture.** Jokes, comments and imagery that sexualise or demean a
  colleague are treated as conduct under this policy, not as harmless atmosphere.

## Reporting routes

A person may report through any of the following. They are never required to report to
the person the report is about, or to that person's manager.

- **To a manager** - any manager, not only the person's own.
- **To the Gender Equality Officer** - by email to **Support@PlayForm.Cloud**, marked
  for the attention of the Gender Equality Officer. This route bypasses line
  management.
- **To a member of the Gender Equality Committee** - any member, whose contact details
  are published internally.
- **Anonymously** - through the confidential reporting channel published internally,
  which accepts a report without a name. An anonymous report is investigated on the
  facts available; the Company states in advance that it may be unable to act if the
  report cannot be verified, and it says so rather than closing the case silently.
- **Through a representative** - a colleague, a staff representative or an external
  adviser may report on a person's behalf, with their consent.
- **Externally** - to the Bulgarian Commission for Protection against Discrimination,
  the labour inspectorate, the police, or the competent judicial authorities. Reporting
  externally does not require internal reporting first, and the Company does not
  require a person to exhaust internal routes.

A report may be made orally or in writing, in the reporter's own words. No form is
required, and a report is not rejected for being incomplete.

## What happens after a report

- **Acknowledgement within two working days.** The person reporting is told who is
  handling the case, what will happen next, and where they can get support.
- **A named handler.** The Gender Equality Officer handles the case, unless the report
  concerns the Officer, in which case a member of top management handles it and the
  Officer takes no part.
- **Conflict check.** A person named in the report, or with a close personal or
  professional relationship to a person named, takes no part in handling it.
- **Recording.** The report, the steps taken, the evidence gathered and the decision
  are recorded in a confidential case file, separate from personnel files.
- **Communication.** The person reporting is kept informed of the progress at agreed
  intervals, and is told the outcome.

## Interim measures and support

The Company acts on the situation before the investigation concludes. At the request
of the person affected, or on the Company's own assessment, it may:

- change reporting lines, teams, schedules or workspace;
- adjust the working arrangements of either person, giving priority to the preferences
  of the person affected;
- grant leave;
- restrict contact between the people concerned;
- suspend the person named, where the risk justifies it and the law allows.

The person affected is offered, at the Company's expense:

- access to psychological support;
- referral to legal advice, including on the criminal and civil routes available;
- information about the external bodies to which they can report;
- a named person inside the Company to deal with practical matters.

Support is offered whether or not the person chooses to pursue an investigation, and
is not conditional on the outcome.

## Investigation and decision

- An investigation begins within five working days of the report.
- The investigator gathers the available evidence: written accounts, messages, records,
  and interviews with the people concerned and with witnesses.
- The person named is informed of the substance of the report, and is given the
  opportunity to respond. Their account is recorded.
- The investigation is concluded, and a written decision issued, **within 30 working
  days**. An extension is possible where the facts require it, is recorded with
  reasons, and is communicated to the person reporting.
- The decision states whether the conduct is found to have occurred, on the balance of
  probabilities, the reasons, and the action to be taken.
- **Appeal.** Either party may appeal in writing within ten working days. The appeal is
  decided by a person not involved in the original decision - top management, or an
  external adviser where top management is conflicted.
- The case file is closed with the outcome, the actions taken and their dates.

## Consequences

Where the conduct is found to have occurred, the response is proportionate to the
conduct and is applied consistently. It may include:

- a recorded warning;
- mandatory training;
- a change of role, team or reporting line;
- loss of a leadership or panel role;
- termination of employment or of the contract;
- a report to the competent authorities where the conduct may be criminal;
- recovery of costs where the law allows.

A finding against a third party results in the matter being raised with their employer
or the partner organisation, and, where the contract allows, in the termination of the
relationship.

## Non-retaliation and confidentiality

- Retaliation against a person who reports, supports a report or participates in an
  investigation is a disciplinary matter, treated as seriously as the original conduct.
- The identity of the person reporting is disclosed only where the investigation or the
  law requires it, and they are told in advance if that happens.
- Confidentiality binds everyone involved in handling the case. A breach of
  confidentiality is itself a disciplinary matter.
- A report made in good faith is protected even if it is not upheld. A knowingly false
  report is a disciplinary matter; a report that cannot be substantiated is not.

## Cooperation with the authorities

Where a report describes conduct that may be criminal, the Company informs the person
affected of their right to report it to the police, supports them in doing so, and
cooperates with any investigation. The Company's own procedure runs in parallel and
does not wait for a criminal outcome, and a criminal acquittal does not by itself
determine the outcome of the Company's procedure.

## Data protection

Case data is personal data, and part of it may be a special category of data. It is
processed on the legal bases set out in the [Personal Data Protection Policy], is
accessible only to those handling the case, is kept separately from personnel files,
and is retained only as long as necessary for the procedure, for the defence of legal
claims, and for the monitoring described below.

## Monitoring

The Gender Equality Officer reports **anonymised** statistics to the Gender Equality
Committee quarterly and in the biennial monitoring report: the number of reports by
route and category, the time to acknowledgement and to conclusion, the outcomes, and
repeat cases. No statistic identifies a person, and small numbers are suppressed.

The Committee reviews the statistics for patterns - a recurring team, setting or type
of conduct - and the pattern triggers a review of prevention, not only of response.

# GENDER DIMENSION IN RESEARCH AND TEACHING POLICY

Effective date: 11.23.2026 / November 23rd 2026\
Last updated: 11.23.2026 / November 23rd 2026

This policy carries the research and teaching part of the
[Gender Equality Plan]. The Company has no students and no teaching programmes, so
the teaching part applies to the technical documentation, examples and training
material the Company publishes - the material through which it teaches its own
product. The research part applies to the Company's research and development work
and to its product design.

## Purpose

Software is not neutral. A dataset records the world as it was measured; a model
learns the pattern it was shown; an interface assumes a user who may not exist. When
sex and gender are left out of that chain, the result works less well for half the
people who use it - and the failure is invisible, because nothing in the product says
what it left out.

Funders increasingly ask how the gender dimension is handled, but the reason to
handle it is not the ask. Software that ignores sex and gender works less well for
the people who use it, and the failure is invisible because nothing in the product
says what it left out. This policy applies the discipline to all of the Company's
work, funded or not.

## Scope

This policy applies to:

- research projects and experiments, internal and funded;
- the design and development of products, features and services;
- datasets, benchmarks, models and evaluation sets the Company builds or uses;
- user research, analytics and telemetry;
- technical documentation, examples, tutorials and training material;
- proposals and reports the Company submits to funders.

## The gender-relevance check

Every research project and every product design cycle begins with a recorded check,
answered by the people doing the work and reviewed by the project lead:

- **Does sex or gender affect the question?** Does the phenomenon being studied,
  measured or built for differ between women and men, or between people of different
  genders?
- **Does it affect the data?** Is the data about people, and does the population it
  covers represent the people the result will be applied to?
- **Does it affect the outcome?** Could the result perform differently, or cause
  different harms or benefits, for different groups?
- **Does it affect the use?** Does the design assume a household, working pattern,
  body, name or role that not everyone has?
- **What will be done about it?** The specific measure, or an explicit, reasoned
  statement that the dimension does not apply.

A "does not apply" answer is legitimate and is recorded with its reason. The failure
this policy prevents is the question never being asked, not the answer being no.

## Research questions and methods

- Where the gender dimension applies, it is stated in the research question and
  carried into the method - sampling, instruments, analysis and interpretation - not
  added as a paragraph in the report.
- Recruitment of research participants aims for a sample that reflects the population
  the result is meant to serve, and reports its composition.
- Analysis distinguishes between sex-disaggregated findings and findings that hold
  across groups, and says which it is reporting.
- Where a study cannot disaggregate, the limitation is stated in the output rather
  than left implicit.

## Data and datasets

- Data about people is collected and published with sex and gender fields where the
  phenomenon differs by sex or gender, with a "not stated" option that is reported
  rather than discarded.
- The composition of a dataset is documented: who is in it, who is missing, and how
  the missing group was excluded.
- Data that is derived from historical records carries a note on the bias in the
  source - for example where a category was collected only for one group, or where
  the labels encode a stereotype.
- Personal data used in research is handled under the
  [Personal Data Protection Policy], including minimisation and the safeguards for
  special categories of data.

## Models, evaluation and benchmarks

- Before release, every model, evaluation set and benchmark the Company publishes or
  relies on receives a **documented bias review**: representation in the training and
  evaluation data, labelling practice, and measured performance differences across
  groups where the data allows measurement.
- A measured difference is either explained, mitigated, or disclosed. It is never
  silently carried into a product.
- Evaluation sets are not reused indefinitely as a proxy for the world; their
  composition is reviewed when the product's user population changes.
- Where the Company uses a third-party model or dataset, the same questions are asked
  of it, and the answer is recorded in the integration decision.

## Product design and interfaces

- Inclusive design review of every user-facing flow: language, imagery, defaults,
  form fields, and assumptions about names, titles, family structure, care
  responsibilities and working patterns.
- Form fields that ask for a title, a gender or a salutation are justified by a stated
  need; where they are not needed, they are removed.
- Content and imagery represent a range of people, and are reviewed for stereotypes
  rather than only for absence.
- Accessibility is treated as part of the same review: an interface that excludes by
  disability excludes by design in the same way.
- Telemetry and analytics distinguish what is measured from what is assumed, and do
  not infer sensitive characteristics.

## Documentation, examples and training material

- Technical documentation, tutorials, examples and training material use inclusive
  language and neutral examples: "the user", "the operator", "the developer" rather
  than a default person.
- Examples that would only make sense for one kind of user are rewritten or
  supplemented.
- Terminology is consistent across the documentation set, and a term that changes is
  changed everywhere rather than in the newest page only.
- Material the Company publishes for its own onboarding follows the same rules, so
  that the standard is learned from the first day.

## Proposals and funded projects

- Every proposal submitted to an external funder from 1 January 2027 addresses the
  gender dimension where the subject or the call makes it relevant, and states how
  it will be addressed in the work.
- The commitment made in the proposal is carried into the project: the check is
  recorded, the measures are delivered, and the result is reported in the project's
  deliverables.
- Where a project is part of a consortium, the Company applies this policy to its own
  work packages and raises the dimension in consortium-level design discussions.

## Records, sign-off and corrective action

- The gender-relevance check, the dataset composition note, the bias review and the
  design review are recorded in the project record - the same place as the technical
  decisions they accompany, so that they are read.
- The project lead signs the record before release. A release without the record is a
  defect in the release process, not an optional omission.
- A review that finds a problem raises a corrective action with an owner and a date;
  the action and its closure are recorded.
- The Gender Equality Officer reviews a sample of records each year and reports the
  findings to the Gender Equality Committee.

## Targets and indicators

- 100% of research projects and product design cycles started from 1 January 2027
  carry a completed gender-relevance check.
- 100% of datasets and models released from 1 January 2027 carry a documented bias
  review.
- Every proposal submitted to an external funder from 1 January 2027 addresses the
  gender dimension explicitly.
- Indicators: share of projects with a completed check; share of releases with a bias
  review; share of proposals addressing the dimension; number of corrective actions
  raised and closed; findings of the annual sample review.

# GENDER EQUALITY PLAN

Effective date: 11.23.2026 / November 23rd 2026\
Last updated: 11.23.2026 / November 23rd 2026

This is the Gender Equality Plan (the **GEP**) of PlayForm ltd. (the **Company**).
It is a formal document, published on the Company's website at a stable address and
adopted by the Company's top management. It states what the Company commits to, who
is accountable for each commitment, how progress is measured, and what evidence the
Company can produce when a partner, a customer, an auditor or a funder asks for it.

The plan is not a statement of intent. Every commitment below names its mechanism,
its owner and its evidence. The five areas the plan covers each carry concrete
measures, targets and indicators, and progress is published at least every two
years.

## Why the Company has this plan

The Company builds software and runs cloud services, and it competes for the people
who do that work. Two things follow. A company that lets ability decide who gets in,
who advances and who is heard gets better work; a company where some people are
quietly slowed down loses them, and loses what they would have built. And the people
the Company wants to hire and keep now ask what it is actually doing, not what it
says it believes.

The plan also answers a question the Company is asked with increasing regularity by
the organisations it works with. Partners, customers and funders expect a supplier
to be able to show a documented, published and measured commitment to gender
equality, not a paragraph of intent. Having one plan, in force and maintained,
answers that question once instead of in every negotiation.

The Company maintains this plan for three reasons:

- **Fairness** - equal treatment is the standard the Company holds itself to,
  independent of who is watching.
- **Substance** - the measures in this plan are the Company's own employment and
  research practice, not a compliance artefact. They are written to be applied.
- **Verifiability** - every commitment in this plan can be checked against a
  document, a record or a published report, as set out in [Annex A].

The plan also implements the Company's commitments under the
[Equality, Diversity and Inclusion Policy] and the equality, labour and
pay-transparency law that applies to it.

## Scope and application

This plan applies to the Company as a legal entity, to all of its personnel
whatever their contract type, to candidates for employment, and to the conduct of
contractors and partners while working for the Company.

The Company has no students and no teaching programmes. The measures in this plan
that concern teaching therefore apply to the technical documentation and training
material the Company publishes, and the measures that concern research apply to its
research and development work and to its product design.

The plan is a Company-level document. It applies wherever the Company's work
happens - office, remote, travel, events, and online - and it applies to
subcontractors to the extent that the Company can contractually require it.

## What the plan commits to

Four commitments make this plan verifiable rather than aspirational: it is published
and adopted at the top, it is resourced, it is measured, and it is taught. Each is
set out below with the mechanism that delivers it and the evidence the Company can
produce on request.

### 1. A published plan, adopted at the top

**The commitment.** This document is the plan. It is published at a stable public
address, and it is adopted by the Company's top management.

**What the Company does.**

- This document is the GEP. It is published at a stable public address -
  PlayForm.Cloud/Gender-Equality-Plan - reachable from the site footer and from
  the [Documentation](/Documentation) register, without authentication and without
  a request.
- It is adopted by the Company's top management. The signed original carries the
  signature and the date; the published version states the version number and the
  date of the last update, so the version in force is always identifiable.
- Supporting evidence of adoption is retained with the signed original: the
  management decision adopting the plan, the appointment of the Gender Equality
  Officer, and the decision establishing the Gender Equality Committee.
- Previous and older versions of the plan are retained and provided on request, so
  that a funder can verify which version was in force at any date during a grant.
- The plan is written in English, the working language of the Company and of its
  work with international partners.

**Accountable.** Managing Director.

### 2. Resources and expertise

**The commitment.** The plan is owned, staffed, budgeted, and supported by external
expertise wherever the Company's own capacity is not sufficient.

**What the Company does.**

- **A named owner.** The Gender Equality Officer (GEO) is formally appointed by
  top management, with the appointment recorded in writing. The role carries
  dedicated working time - not an additional duty squeezed into another role -
  and a defined mandate: coordinate the measures, run the data collection, prepare
  the monitoring reports, advise managers, and act as a confidential reporting
  route.
- **A standing body.** The Gender Equality Committee (GEC) is established by
  management decision, meets at least quarterly, keeps minutes, and includes top
  management, the GEO and representatives of the personnel. Its minutes are the
  evidence that the plan is being run, not shelved.
- **A budget.** The plan has its own budget line, covering the GEO's time, external
  gender-equality expertise, training delivery, the biennial monitoring report, and
  the reasonable accommodations and adjustments the plan commits to. The budget is
  approved with the plan and reviewed annually.
- **External expertise.** Where the Company's own capacity is not sufficient - for
  example for the pay-gap analysis, the review of research content, or the
  investigation of a complex case - the Company contracts external expertise. The
  contracts are retained as evidence.
- **Time for the people involved.** Members of the GEC and of recruitment panels
  have their participation recognised as working time, and the training required by
  this plan is delivered during working hours.

**Accountable.** Managing Director, with the GEO as day-to-day owner.

### 3. Measurement and reporting

**The commitment.** The Company measures its own position against defined
indicators and publishes the result at least every two years.

**What the Company does.**

- The Company collects sex and gender disaggregated data on headcount, contract
  type, role and grade, pay, recruitment, promotion, training, leave and exits,
  through the process, definitions and safeguards fixed in the
  [Gender Equality Data and Monitoring Framework].
- The indicators are defined in [Annex B] of this plan, with a formula for each, so
  that a figure can be reproduced rather than taken on trust.
- The Company publishes a **Gender Equality Monitoring Report at least every two
  years**. The first baseline report is due by 30 June 2027; subsequent reports are
  due in 2029, 2031 and so on, and are published in the
  [Documentation](/Documentation) register.
- Between reports, the GEC reviews the data at least annually, and records the
  review in its minutes.
- Small numbers are suppressed in published tables to protect individuals, and the
  suppression rule is stated in each report.
- Data is collected and processed in accordance with the
  [Personal Data Protection Policy]; participation in a monitoring questionnaire is
  never a condition of employment, and "prefer not to say" is always an available
  answer.

**Accountable.** Gender Equality Officer, with the People and Culture function.

### 4. Training and awareness

**The commitment.** Everyone is trained, and the people who decide are trained more.

**What the Company does.**

- The [Gender Equality Training and Awareness Programme] defines a curriculum for
  three distinct audiences: **all personnel**, **managers and decision-makers**, and
  **members of recruitment and promotion panels**. The requirement to target both
  staff and decision-makers is met by design, not by a single general session.
- Content includes unconscious bias, inclusive language, the standards in the
  [Equality, Diversity and Inclusion Policy], the reporting routes, and - for
  managers - bias in evaluation, pay and promotion decisions.
- Decision-makers complete a longer module than the general population, and panel
  members complete their module **before** sitting on a panel.
- The programme is scheduled annually, delivered during working hours, and open to
  all personnel regardless of contract type or working pattern.
- Evidence retained: the curriculum, the training plan, invitations and programmes,
  attendance records, and the contracts of external experts where training is
  outsourced.

**Accountable.** Gender Equality Officer, with the Managing Director for
decision-maker participation.

## The five areas the plan addresses

The plan covers five areas, each with concrete measures and targets. Each area below
states its objective, its measures, its targets and indicators, its owner, and the
policy that carries the detail.

### 1. Work-life balance and organisational culture

**Objective.** Make it possible to combine the Company's work with care
responsibilities and a life outside work, and remove the cultural signals that make
some people appear more committed than others.

**Measures.**

- Flexible working by default: core collaboration hours, flexible start and end of
  day, hybrid and fully remote arrangements, and part-time arrangements that are
  not a career penalty.
- Predictable scheduling: recurring meetings inside a defined window, agendas
  circulated in advance, meetings recorded or minuted where someone cannot attend,
  and focus time protected from meetings.
- Leave for care: the full statutory entitlements, plus company enhancements
  defined in the [Work-Life Balance and Organisational Culture Policy] - paid carer
  days, leave for reproductive-health appointments, and a phased return from
  parental leave on full pay.
- A return guarantee: the same role, or a role of equal value, on return from
  parental leave, with any change agreed in writing before the leave begins.
- Culture: inclusive language, alcohol-free alternatives at Company events, events
  scheduled inside working hours where possible, workload monitoring, and an
  explicit rule that overtime and presence are not proxies for performance.

**Targets.** 100% of managers trained in managing flexible and part-time work by
31 December 2027. 100% of parental-leave returns accompanied by a written return
plan from 1 January 2027. Zero meetings scheduled outside the collaboration window
except by agreement of all participants, measured by quarterly sampling.

**Indicators.** Take-up of flexible and part-time arrangements by sex; parental
leave taken by sex; return-to-role rate after parental leave; voluntary exit rate
within twelve months of returning; overtime hours by sex.

**Owner.** People and Culture function. **Detail.** [Work-Life Balance and
Organisational Culture Policy].

### 2. Gender balance in leadership and decision-making

**Objective.** Reach a balanced composition in the bodies that decide, so that
decisions about the Company's work are not made by a single group.

**Measures.**

- A representation target for every decision-making body: the management team, the
  Gender Equality Committee, recruitment and promotion panels, project steering
  groups, and the programme committees of Company events.
- Balanced shortlists: where the candidate pool allows, at least one candidate of
  each sex on every shortlist for a leadership role.
- Balanced panels: no panel that decides on a person's recruitment, promotion or pay
  is composed of a single sex where the Company can avoid it.
- Sponsorship and succession: each member of the management team sponsors at least
  one person from an under-represented group, with a named successor for every
  critical role.
- Transparency: appointments to leadership roles are announced internally with the
  criteria applied.

**Targets.** 40% of the under-represented sex in the management team and in every
decision-making body by 31 December 2028, with an interim milestone of 30% by
31 December 2027. 50% women among the speakers at Company-organised events from
1 January 2027, counting only events with a speaker programme.

**Indicators.** Composition of each decision-making body by sex; share of balanced
shortlists; share of balanced panels; share of women among speakers; share of
sponsored people who move into a leadership role within two years.

**Owner.** Managing Director. **Detail.** [Gender Balance in Leadership and
Decision-Making Policy].

### 3. Gender equality in recruitment and career progression

**Objective.** Make hiring, pay and promotion decisions rest on documented,
job-related criteria, and remove the mechanisms through which bias enters them.

**Measures.**

- Gender-neutral job titles, adverts and requirements, reviewed before publication;
  no requirement for a salary history; no unnecessary degree or experience
  thresholds that narrow the pool without a job-related reason.
- Structured selection: the same questions for every candidate for a role, a written
  scoring rubric agreed before interviews begin, and a panel of at least two people
  trained under this plan.
- Pay equity: equal pay for equal work and work of equal value, an annual pay-gap
  analysis by grade and by role family, and a remediation plan with a budget
  whenever an unexplained gap is found. The Company prepares for the obligations of
  Directive (EU) 2023/970, including the right to pay information and joint pay
  assessments.
- Promotion: published criteria, one annual calibration across all managers, and a
  rule that statutory leave - maternity, paternity, parental, sick, carer's - does
  not count against time in role or against performance ratings.
- Equal access to development: training budget, conference attendance and stretch
  assignments are allocated by criteria and tracked by sex, so that opportunity does
  not accumulate informally.
- Exit analysis: every voluntary exit is reviewed for signals of unequal treatment,
  and the findings feed the biennial report.

**Targets.** 100% of vacancies advertised with a gender-neutral advert and scored
against a written rubric from 1 January 2027. An annual pay-gap analysis published
in the monitoring report. A promotion parity ratio between 0.9 and 1.1 in every
reporting cycle, with a written explanation whenever it falls outside that band.

**Indicators.** Applicants, shortlisted and appointed by sex at each stage of the
funnel; promotion rate by sex and grade; mean and median pay gap by grade and role
family; training spend per person by sex; voluntary exit rate by sex.

**Owner.** People and Culture function. **Detail.** [Recruitment and Career
Progression Policy].

### 4. Gender dimension in research and teaching content

**Objective.** Make the Company's research, product design, data and documentation
attentive to sex and gender, so that what the Company builds works for the people
who use it and does not encode bias.

**Measures.**

- A gender-relevance check at the start of every research project and product
  design cycle: does sex or gender affect the question, the data, the users, the
  outcomes or the harms?
- Sex and gender disaggregated data wherever the phenomenon being studied or
  measured differs by sex or gender, with the reason recorded when it does not.
- Bias review of datasets, models, evaluation sets and benchmarks before release,
  including representation, labelling and performance differences.
- Inclusive design review of user interfaces and product flows, including language,
  imagery, defaults and assumptions about household, care and work patterns.
- Review of technical documentation, examples and training material for inclusive
  language and for examples that assume a single kind of user.
- For proposals to external funders: the gender dimension is addressed in the
  proposal wherever the subject or the call makes it relevant, and the plan for
  doing so is written into the proposal and carried into the project.

**Targets.** 100% of research projects and product design cycles from 1 January 2027
carry a completed gender-relevance check in their project record. 100% of released
datasets and models from 1 January 2027 carry a documented bias review. Every
research proposal submitted to an external funder from 1 January 2027 addresses the
gender dimension explicitly.

**Indicators.** Share of projects with a completed check; share of releases with a
bias review; share of proposals addressing the gender dimension; number of
corrective actions arising from reviews.

**Owner.** Research and engineering leads, with the GEO. **Detail.**
[Gender Dimension in Research and Teaching Policy].

### 5. Measures against gender-based violence, including sexual harassment

**Objective.** Prevent gender-based violence and sexual harassment, respond to it
effectively when it occurs, and support the person affected.

**Measures.**

- A published policy with definitions, a zero-tolerance statement and a procedure
  with fixed timelines, covering the workplace, remote work, business travel,
  Company events and work-related online channels.
- Multiple reporting routes, including a route that bypasses the person's manager
  and a confidential channel that allows anonymous reporting.
- A response standard: acknowledgement within two working days, interim protective
  measures where needed, a documented investigation, a decision, and an appeal
  route.
- Prevention: annual training for all personnel, a specific module for managers,
  a risk assessment for off-site events and travel, and a contractual clause
  requiring contractors and partners to meet the same standard.
- Support for the person affected: access to psychological support, legal referral,
  leave, and adjustments to working arrangements at their request.
- Anonymised case statistics - reports received, routes used, outcomes and time to
  resolution - published in the biennial monitoring report.

**Targets.** 100% of reports acknowledged within two working days. 100% of
investigations concluded within 30 working days or with a documented extension.
100% of personnel trained annually. The contractual clause in place with all new
contractors from 1 January 2027.

**Indicators.** Number of reports by route and by category; time to acknowledgement
and to conclusion; outcomes; repeat cases; training completion.

**Owner.** Gender Equality Officer. **Detail.** [Gender-Based Violence and Sexual
Harassment Policy].

## Targets and dates

- **31 December 2026** - Gender Equality Officer appointed; Gender Equality
  Committee established and first meeting held; budget line approved; this plan
  published; baseline data collection started.
- **31 January 2027** - baseline dataset complete for the 2026 reference year.
- **30 June 2027** - first Gender Equality Monitoring Report published.
- **31 December 2027** - interim milestone: 30% of the under-represented sex in
  every decision-making body; all managers trained in flexible and part-time work;
  all vacancies advertised and scored under the new recruitment standard; all
  research and product cycles carrying a gender-relevance check; the contractual
  clause on gender-based violence in all new contracts.
- **31 December 2028** - 40% of the under-represented sex in the management team and
  in every decision-making body; promotion parity ratio inside the 0.9-1.1 band or
  explained; mid-term review of this plan completed.
- **2029** - second biennial report; revision of this plan on the basis of the
  first full cycle of evidence.

## Governance and responsibilities

**Managing Director** - adopts the plan, appoints the Gender Equality Officer,
approves the budget, signs the monitoring reports, and is accountable for the
leadership target.

**Gender Equality Officer** - owns the plan and its evidence file, coordinates the
measures, runs the data collection, drafts the monitoring reports, advises managers,
and receives confidential reports.

**Gender Equality Committee** - meets at least quarterly, reviews progress and
anonymised case statistics, proposes corrective action, and records all of it in
minutes.

**People and Culture function** - maintains the personnel data, produces the
disaggregated dataset, runs the recruitment and promotion standards, and keeps the
training records.

**Managers** - apply the measures in their own teams, complete the decision-maker
training, and act on concerns raised with them.

**All personnel** - complete the annual training, use the reporting routes, and hold
the plan to its word by raising it when it is not being followed.

## Implementation timetable

**Phase 1 - establish (November 2026 to January 2027).** Appoint the GEO; establish
the GEC; approve the budget; publish this plan and the supporting policies; start
baseline data collection; commission the external expertise the plan depends on.

**Phase 2 - apply (2027).** Roll out the training programme; introduce the
recruitment, promotion and pay standards; introduce the gender-relevance check into
research and product cycles; publish the first monitoring report; reach the interim
representation milestone.

**Phase 3 - consolidate (2028).** Reach the representation target; run the mid-term
review; act on the findings of the first report; extend the contractor clause to all
suppliers.

**Phase 4 - revise (2029 onwards).** Publish the second report; revise the plan and
its targets on the basis of two years of evidence; continue the two-year reporting
cycle.

## Monitoring, evaluation and reporting

The GEC reviews progress against every target at least quarterly and records the
review in its minutes. The GEO prepares an annual internal review for top
management, comparing the indicators in [Annex B] against the baseline and against
the targets above.

The Company publishes a **Gender Equality Monitoring Report at least every two
years**, containing: the indicator values for the reporting period; progress against
each target with an explanation where a target was missed; the pay-gap analysis; the
anonymised statistics on reports of gender-based violence and sexual harassment;
the training completion rates; and the corrective actions decided.

The report is published in the [Documentation](/Documentation) register, and previous
reports remain available so that progress can be traced over time.

## Review, versioning and published versions

This plan is reviewed annually and fully revised after every biennial report. Each
version carries a version number and a date, and states what changed. The version
published at PlayForm.Cloud/Gender-Equality-Plan is the version in force; the signed
original of each version is retained by the Company.

A revision is required, not optional, when: the Company's workforce or structure
changes materially; a target is missed by more than one reporting cycle; the
applicable law changes; or the monitoring data shows a gap the plan does not
address.

## Annex B - Indicators

Each indicator is reported by sex, and where relevant by grade, contract type and
working pattern, for the reference period.

- **Headcount composition** - number and share of personnel by sex, grade, contract
  type and working pattern, at the end of the reference period.
- **Leadership composition** - share of each decision-making body held by each sex.
- **Applicant funnel** - applicants, shortlisted candidates and appointments by sex
  at each stage; the conversion rate between stages by sex.
- **Promotion rate** - promotions divided by headcount, by sex and grade.
- **Promotion parity ratio** - promotion rate of the under-represented sex divided
  by the promotion rate of the other sex; 1.0 means parity.
- **Gender pay gap** - the difference between the mean (and separately the median)
  pay of women and men, as a percentage of the mean (median) pay of men, computed on
  base pay and again on total pay, by grade and by role family.
- **Training participation** - hours and spend per person by sex; completion rate of
  the mandatory modules by audience.
- **Leave** - parental, paternity, carer's and sick leave taken, by sex, and the
  return-to-role rate afterwards.
- **Voluntary exits** - exit rate by sex, and the share of exits within twelve months
  of returning from parental leave.
- **Flexible working** - take-up of part-time, remote and compressed arrangements by
  sex and grade.
- **Reports of gender-based violence and sexual harassment** - number of reports by
  route and category, time to acknowledgement, time to conclusion, outcomes, and
  repeat cases.
- **Research and product reviews** - share of projects with a completed
  gender-relevance check; share of released datasets and models with a documented
  bias review.

## Annex C - Glossary

**Gender Equality Plan (GEP)** - a formal document that sets out an organisation's
commitment to gender equality, the measures it takes, the resources it commits, the
data it collects and the training it delivers, adopted by top management and
published on the organisation's website.

**Under-represented sex** - in a given body, grade or role, the sex that holds less
than half of the positions; the target applies to that sex, whichever it is in that
context.

**Sex and gender disaggregated data** - data reported separately for women and men,
and where the Company's data collection allows, for people who record a gender other
than the one assigned at birth or who prefer not to state one.

**Gender dimension in research content** - the consideration of sex and gender as
variables in the questions asked, the data used, the methods applied, the results
interpreted and the products designed.

**Gender-based violence** - violence directed against a person because of their
gender, or violence that affects persons of a particular gender disproportionately,
including sexual harassment, in person and online.

**Positive action** - a proportionate, time-limited measure that addresses an
existing under-representation or disadvantage, without displacing merit as the basis
of an individual decision.

RECRUITMENT AND CAREER PROGRESSION POLICY

Effective date: 11.23.2026 / November 23rd 2026
Last updated: 11.23.2026 / November 23rd 2026

This policy carries the recruitment and career progression part of the Gender Equality Plan. It governs the three decisions that shape a working life - whether someone is hired, what they are paid, and whether they advance - and it fixes the process for each so that the decision can be reviewed afterwards.

v. 1.0.

This policy shall be reviewed annually, and each time the Company's grading, pay structure or legal obligations change.

Purpose

Unstructured hiring and informal promotion are the two most reliable ways for bias to enter an organisation: they leave no record, so they cannot be examined, and they reward the candidate who resembles the last appointment. The Company replaces both with a documented process whose criteria are written before the decision is made.

Scope

This policy applies to every vacancy, every engagement of a contractor for a role equivalent to employment, every promotion, every pay decision and every internal assignment. It applies to all personnel, including part-time, fixed-term, remote and agency personnel, and to the panels and managers who make these decisions.

Job design and advertising

  • Job titles are gender-neutral, and so is the advert. Terms that carry a gendered connotation, or that code the role as masculine or feminine, are removed at review.
  • Requirements are job-related. A degree, a number of years of experience or a particular technology is required only where the role genuinely needs it, because inflated requirements narrow the pool without improving the hire.
  • Every advert states the grade or salary band, or the range within which an offer will be made. Candidates are not asked for a salary history, and a refusal to provide one is never treated as a negative signal.
  • Adverts state that flexible and remote arrangements are available where they are, and name the Company’s equality commitments with a link to this register.
  • Every advert is reviewed against this section before publication.

Selection

  • Structured process. The same stages, the same questions and the same exercise for every candidate for a role.
  • Written rubric. Scoring criteria are agreed and recorded before the first interview. Scores are recorded individually before the panel discusses them, so that the loudest voice does not set the outcome.
  • Trained panels. Every panel member has completed the recruitment module of the Gender Equality Training and Awareness Programme. No panel decides alone: at least two people score every candidate.
  • Balanced panels. No panel is composed of a single sex where the Company can avoid it, and a panel that is not balanced records why.
  • Reasonable accommodation at every stage. Adjustments for disability, caring responsibilities or religious observance are offered in the invitation, not only on request.
  • Feedback. Every interviewed candidate receives feedback on request, based on the rubric.
  • Records. The advert, the rubric, the individual scores and the panel composition are retained, so that a decision can be reviewed afterwards.

Pay equity and pay transparency

  • The Company applies equal pay for equal work and for work of equal value. Pay is set by grade and role family, not by negotiation outcome.
  • A pay-gap analysis is carried out annually, on base pay and on total pay, by grade and by role family, using both the mean and the median. The results are published in the biennial monitoring report.
  • Where the analysis shows an unexplained gap, the Company adopts a remediation plan with a budget and a date, and reports the outcome in the following report.
  • The Company prepares for Directive (EU) 2023/970 on pay transparency: candidates receive pay information before interview; the pay structure is documented; a joint pay assessment is carried out where the directive requires one; and workers are informed of the criteria used to determine pay and progression.
  • Starting pay is set from the band for the grade. Where a candidate’s expectation is above the band, the role or the grade is reconsidered rather than the individual’s pay being set by negotiation.

Promotion

  • Promotion criteria are published, and state what is expected at the next grade.
  • Promotions are decided once a year in a calibration across all managers, so that a person’s chances do not depend on which manager they happen to report to.
  • Promotion panels are balanced and trained, and use the same rubric discipline as recruitment.
  • Statutory leave does not count against time in role. Maternity, paternity, parental, adoption, sick and carer’s leave are neutral in promotion decisions, and objectives are pro-rated for the period of leave.
  • Part-time and remote personnel are eligible on the same terms. Where a role at the next grade is advertised as full-time only, the reason is recorded.
  • A manager’s promotion decisions are reviewed for skew by sex as part of the annual calibration.

Development, assignment and visibility

  • Training budget, conference attendance, certification and stretch assignments are allocated by published criteria, and spend is tracked by sex and grade.
  • Opportunities are announced to everyone rather than offered to a circle; the Company keeps a record of who was invited to what.
  • Internal mobility - moving between teams, products or disciplines - is open to everyone, and a move never requires a justification about family circumstances.
  • Conference speaking, open-source maintainership and external visibility are treated as development opportunities and allocated on the same basis.

Part-time, fixed-term, agency and remote personnel

  • Pay, grade, development budget, promotion eligibility and access to training are pro-rated, not reduced.
  • Fixed-term and agency personnel are included in the Company’s training, in the equality data collection where the law allows, and in the culture of the team they work in.
  • No decision about a person’s future is made on the basis of their working pattern rather than their work.

Exits

Every voluntary exit is reviewed by the People and Culture function for signals of unequal treatment: the reason given, the grade and sex of the person, the timing relative to leave or a promotion decision, and any concern raised before the exit. Findings are aggregated and reported in the biennial monitoring report, and a pattern triggers a review of the relevant process.

Targets and indicators

  • 100% of vacancies advertised with a gender-neutral advert and scored against a written rubric from 1 January 2027.
  • 100% of interview panels trained and balanced from 1 January 2027, with reasons recorded where a panel could not be balanced.
  • An annual pay-gap analysis published in the monitoring report, with a remediation plan whenever an unexplained gap is found.
  • A promotion parity ratio between 0.9 and 1.1 in every reporting cycle, with a written explanation when it falls outside that band.
  • Indicators: applicants, shortlisted and appointed by sex at each stage; promotion rate by sex and grade; mean and median pay gap by grade and role family; training spend per person by sex; voluntary exit rate by sex; share of part-time personnel by sex and grade.

GENDER EQUALITY TRAINING AND AWARENESS PROGRAMME

Effective date: 11.23.2026 / November 23rd 2026
Last updated: 11.23.2026 / November 23rd 2026

This programme carries the training part of the Gender Equality Plan: awareness raising and training for everyone, and a longer curriculum for the people who decide. It defines who is trained, on what, by whom, how often, and what evidence the Company keeps.

Purpose

The other documents in this framework describe decisions and procedures. This one addresses the person making the decision: the manager who reads a CV, the panel that scores an interview, the lead who allocates the interesting work. Training does not replace a structured process - the process is what makes the decision reviewable - but a structured process applied by people who have never examined their own shortcuts still produces biased outcomes.

The programme has two audiences, staff and decision-makers, and it is built so that both are targeted explicitly, with a distinct curriculum for each.

Audiences

  • All personnel - everyone covered by the Equality, Diversity and Inclusion Policy, whatever their contract type, including part-time, fixed-term, remote and agency personnel.
  • Managers and decision-makers - the management team, anyone who manages people, anyone who sits on a recruitment, promotion or pay panel, and the members of the Gender Equality Committee.
  • New joiners - during onboarding, before their first month ends.
  • Third parties where practicable - contractors and partners working alongside the Company’s teams are offered the awareness module, and their contractual terms require them to meet the standard it teaches.

Curriculum

Module 1 - Awareness (all personnel, 2 hours, annual).

  • What gender equality means in this Company, and why the Company has a plan.
  • Unconscious bias: how shortcuts form, where they show up in work, and what reduces their effect.
  • Inclusive language and everyday conduct, with examples drawn from the Company’s own communication.
  • The reporting routes for discrimination, harassment and gender-based violence, and what happens after a report.
  • The Company’s standards on work-life balance: flexible work, leave, and the rule that presence is not performance.

Module 2 - Decision-making (managers and panel members, 6 hours, annual).

  • Bias in evaluation: how the same evidence is read differently depending on who presents it, and what structured scoring does about it.
  • Recruitment: job design, rubric construction, questioning, scoring and feedback.
  • Promotion and pay decisions: calibration, the effect of leave and part-time work, and how to read a pay-gap analysis.
  • Leadership: sponsorship, allocation of stretch work, and the composition of the bodies a manager controls.
  • Handling a report: first response, escalation, confidentiality and non-retaliation.
  • Case work: short scenarios worked through in groups, including a recruitment panel and a disclosure of harassment.

Module 3 - Panel readiness (before sitting on a panel, 3 hours, once).

  • The Company’s recruitment and promotion process, end to end.
  • The rubric, the individual scoring rule and the discussion discipline.
  • Practising a scored interview and reviewing the panel’s own scoring for skew.

Module 4 - Committee and Officer development (annual, external).

  • External training or certification for the Gender Equality Officer and the members of the Gender Equality Committee, covering investigation practice, data analysis and the legal framework.

Decision-makers and panel members

  • Module 2 is a condition of holding a people-management role: a manager who has not completed it in the current cycle does not sit on a panel or make a pay decision until they have.
  • Module 3 is completed before a person’s first panel, and refreshed every two years.
  • The Managing Director and the management team complete Module 2 in the first cycle after adoption, so that the standard is visibly held at the top.

Delivery

  • Modules 1 and 3 may be delivered internally by trained personnel or by an external provider; Module 2 and Module 4 are delivered or co-delivered by external expertise.
  • Delivery is during working hours, and participation is recorded as working time.
  • Sessions are recorded where the format allows, and the recording is available to anyone who could not attend, so that a working pattern is never a barrier to completion.
  • Material is reviewed for inclusive language and current examples before each cycle, under the Gender Dimension in Research and Teaching Policy.
  • The curriculum, the slides and the exercises are retained as the programme’s evidence.

Schedule and completion targets

  • The first full cycle runs in the first half of 2027, following adoption of this framework.
  • All personnel: one Module 1 session per calendar year; 90% completion target per year, with the shortfall named and explained in the monitoring report.
  • Managers and decision-makers: 100% completion of Module 2 within six months of adoption, and annually afterwards.
  • Panel members: 100% completion of Module 3 before sitting.
  • New joiners: Module 1 within 30 days of starting.
  • The Gender Equality Officer maintains the schedule and reports completion to the Gender Equality Committee each quarter.

Evidence and records

The Company retains, and can produce on request:

  • the curriculum and the training material for each module;
  • the annual training plan and the schedule of sessions;
  • invitations, agendas and programmes for each session;
  • attendance records, by module, with the date and the audience;
  • completion certificates or internal records of completion;
  • contracts and credentials of external experts and providers;
  • the evaluation results described below.

These are the records a funder or validator asks for when it verifies the training requirement, and they are kept with the plan’s evidence file rather than in a separate archive.

Evaluation

  • Each module ends with a short knowledge check, completed before and after the session, so that the change in understanding is measurable rather than assumed.
  • Participants rate relevance and quality, and the results are reviewed by the Gender Equality Committee.
  • Behaviour indicators are tracked alongside the training data: the share of balanced panels, the share of balanced shortlists, the promotion parity ratio and the pay gap. Training that does not move any of them is a curriculum problem, and the Committee treats it as one.
  • The Committee records, each cycle, what changed in the curriculum as a result of the evaluation.

Budget

The programme has its own budget line, covering external delivery, materials, the recordings, the Committee and Officer development, and the time of the people attending. The budget is approved with the Gender Equality Plan and reviewed annually.

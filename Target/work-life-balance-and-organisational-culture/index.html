WORK-LIFE BALANCE AND ORGANISATIONAL CULTURE POLICY

Effective date: 11.23.2026 / November 23rd 2026
Last updated: 11.23.2026 / November 23rd 2026

This policy carries the work-life balance and organisational culture part of the Gender Equality Plan. It exists because the way work is organised decides who can do it. When the working day has no edges, when meetings are called at short notice, when leave is quietly counted against someone’s commitment, the people who carry care responsibilities - still disproportionately women - are pushed out or slowed down without anyone deciding to do it.

Purpose and principles

The Company measures work by what is delivered, not by when someone is at a keyboard. Three principles follow, and every rule below is an application of one of them:

  • Outcome over presence. Availability during a fixed window is a coordination device, not a measure of contribution.
  • Predictability. People can plan their lives around their work, and can plan their work around their lives.
  • No penalty for care. Taking leave, working part-time or working remotely never counts against someone in a pay, promotion or assignment decision.

Scope

This policy applies to all personnel, whatever their contract type and working pattern, and to managers in every team. It applies to the Company’s own work and to work done for partners and clients, and it is written so that client expectations are met through planning rather than through permanent availability.

Flexible and remote working

  • Core collaboration hours. The Company works with a core window during which people can be expected to be reachable and meetings can be scheduled. Outside that window, work is asynchronous by default.
  • Flexible start and end. Start and end times may be shifted within the day, subject to the core window and to any legal limit on daily and weekly working time.
  • Remote and hybrid. Remote work is a normal arrangement, not a concession. Fully remote arrangements are available where the role allows it, and are agreed in writing with the schedule and the review date.
  • Part-time. Part-time arrangements are available at every level, including leadership roles. A part-time role carries the same grade, the same pro-rata pay, the same development budget and the same promotion eligibility as a full-time role.
  • Compressed hours. Where the role allows, the same weekly hours may be worked over fewer days.
  • No justification requirement. A request for flexibility is not required to explain why. It is assessed against the operational requirements of the role, and a refusal is given in writing with the reason and the review date.

Working time, meetings and availability

  • Meetings are scheduled inside the core collaboration window except where every participant agrees otherwise.
  • Every recurring meeting has an agenda circulated in advance, a stated purpose and a stated end time. A meeting without an agenda may be declined.
  • Meetings are recorded or minuted where a participant cannot attend, so that attendance is not the only way to be informed.
  • Focus time is protected: teams designate blocks in which no meetings are scheduled.
  • Communication channels have stated expectations: which channel is for urgent matters, and what response time is expected on the others. No one is expected to answer outside their working hours.
  • Notifications outside working hours are queued, not escalated.

Parental, paternity and adoption leave

  • Statutory entitlements are met in full, as provided by the Bulgarian Labour Code and applicable European Union law, including maternity, paternity, parental and adoption leave.
  • Company enhancements. Beyond the statutory minimum, the Company provides a phased return from parental leave: the first month after return is worked at reduced hours on full pay, with the schedule agreed before the leave begins.
  • Return guarantee. A person returning from parental leave returns to the same role, or to a role of equal value and grade where the original role no longer exists, with any change agreed in writing before the leave begins.
  • No detriment. Time on statutory leave does not count against time in role, promotion eligibility, performance rating, bonus reference period or assignment allocation. Objectives are pro-rated for the period of leave.
  • Paternity and second-parent leave is actively encouraged: it is announced in the same terms as maternity leave, and taking it is never recorded as an absence that affects a review.
  • Keeping in touch. The Company offers optional keep-in-touch contact during leave, at the person’s choice, with no expectation of work.

Care responsibilities

  • Paid carer’s leave. Five working days per year, paid, for the care of a dependent child, a dependent adult or an elderly relative, including for the arrangement of care and for emergencies.
  • Emergency leave. Immediate time off for a family emergency, agreed with the manager as soon as practicable and never refused for a genuine emergency.
  • Flexibility for care. Appointments, school schedules and care arrangements are accommodated through flexible hours and remote work without requiring a formal leave request.
  • Men and care. The Company monitors the take-up of care-related leave by sex. Where take-up is heavily skewed, that is treated as a signal about culture, not about need.

Health, pregnancy and reproductive health

  • Pregnancy. Risk assessment of the role on notification of pregnancy; adjustment of duties, travel and schedule where needed; time off for antenatal appointments.
  • Reproductive health. Two paid working days per year for appointments related to fertility treatment, pregnancy loss, menopause or other reproductive-health needs, taken without a requirement to give the reason beyond the manager’s need to plan.
  • Menstrual and menopause support. Flexible hours, remote work and adjustments to workspace and schedule on request.
  • Return after pregnancy loss. Leave and support agreed with the person, without a fixed template, and with no requirement to disclose details to the wider team.
  • Chronic illness and disability. Adjustments under the reasonable-accommodation provision of the Equality, Diversity and Inclusion Policy.

Organisational culture

  • Inclusive language in internal communication, job titles, documentation and product copy, following the review described in the Gender Dimension in Research and Teaching Policy.
  • Events. Company events are scheduled inside working hours where possible, give notice long enough for people with care responsibilities to arrange cover, offer alcohol-free alternatives as an equal option, and never make participation in evening or off-site activity a condition of belonging.
  • Social norms. Informal networks are where opportunity often accumulates. The Company therefore rotates who leads internal sessions, publishes internal opportunities to everyone rather than to a circle, and tracks who is invited to what.
  • Recognition. Recognition is given for outcomes, not for visible hours, and awards and praise are reviewed for skew by sex.

Workload, overtime and burnout

  • Workload is reviewed in every one-to-one, and a sustained overload is treated as a management problem to solve, not an individual failing.
  • Overtime is recorded, is voluntary, is compensated as required by law, and is monitored by sex so that care responsibilities do not silently become a career penalty.
  • A person may raise workload, stress or burnout with their manager or with the Gender Equality Officer without it being recorded as a performance concern.
  • Sustained overtime in a team triggers a review of staffing, scope and deadlines rather than an expectation of endurance.

Requests, approvals and records

A request under this policy is made to the person’s manager and answered within ten working days. Approvals and refusals are recorded in writing, with the reason for a refusal and the date of review. The People and Culture function keeps the record that the indicators below are computed from, with the confidentiality safeguards in the Gender Equality Data and Monitoring Framework.

Targets and indicators

  • 100% of managers trained in managing flexible, part-time and remote work by 31 December 2027.
  • 100% of returns from parental leave accompanied by a written return plan from 1 January 2027.
  • Zero meetings scheduled outside the collaboration window without the agreement of all participants, measured by quarterly sampling.
  • Indicators: take-up of flexible, remote and part-time arrangements by sex and grade; parental, paternity and carer’s leave taken by sex; return-to-role rate; voluntary exits within twelve months of returning; recorded overtime by sex.
